# overlapping-regional-element-clustering-crem

## Summary

Groups REMs inferred by the StitchIt algorithm into Clusters of Regulatory EleMents (CREMs) by merging REMs that overlap or are directly adjacent, assigning each CREM a unique ID. This aggregates per-gene regulatory regions from the EpiRegio database into a coarser, navigable set of regulatory loci (365,286 CREMs in the published build).

## When to use

Use when StitchIt has produced REMs per gene (from paired DNase1-seq and RNA-seq samples) and you need to consolidate overlapping or directly adjacent REMs into CREMs with unique IDs for database-level annotation or counting.

## When NOT to use

- Input is a set of peaks or REMs not derived from the StitchIt gene-centric linear model (e.g. plain DNase1-seq peak calls), where overlap-merging into EpiRegio CREMs would not correspond to the published definition
- You need cell type- or tissue-specific REM activity rather than consolidated CREM loci — EpiRegio's Cell type score and Cell type DNase1 signal operate at the REM level

## Inputs

- REMs with regression coefficients from the STITCHIT linear model, aggregated across all 166 samples
- REM-to-gene assignments (protein-coding and non-protein-coding genes, GENCODE V27 annotation)
- REM genomic coordinates on the 100 kb-upstream / gene body / 100 kb-downstream search window

## Outputs

- CREMs (Clusters of Regulatory EleMents), each with a unique CREM ID and containing a minimum of two REMs
- Database-level summary statistics including total CREM count (reference: 365,286), recorded e.g. in stitchit_database_metrics.csv

## How to apply

After StitchIt has identified REMs by fitting its linear model of depth-normalized DNase1-seq signal versus gene expression (transcripts per million) over each gene's 100,000 bp upstream / gene body / 100,000 bp downstream search window, aggregate REMs across all 166 Roadmap+Blueprint samples and assign each REM to its associated gene. Merge REMs that overlap or are directly adjacent into a single CREM, applying the EpiRegio rule that a CREM must contain a minimum of two REMs, and assign each cluster a unique CREM ID. Rationale: grouping yields loci that represent contiguous regulatory activity rather than many fragmented accessibility-correlated regions, matching the gene-centric EpiRegio design that starts with a gene and looks for REMs. Validate the clustering by comparing the resulting CREM count against the published reference value of 365,286 CREMs alongside the 2,404,861 REMs and 35,379 associated genes.

## Related tools

- **STITCHIT** (Upstream algorithm that infers REMs by interpreting DNase1-seq accessibility variation with respect to gene expression; the REMs it produces are the elements merged into CREMs)
- **EpiRegio** (Web server and database that stores and serves the REMs and CREMs generated by StitchIt; CREMs with unique IDs are part of the EpiRegioDB resource) — github.com/TeamRegio/EpiRegioDB

## Evaluation signals

- Total CREM count matches the published reference of 365,286 CREMs
- Total REM count underlying the clustering matches 2,404,861 REMs across 35,379 genes
- Every emitted CREM contains a minimum of two REMs and carries a unique CREM ID
- Every REM is assigned to exactly its associated gene before clustering (no cross-gene merges)
- Mean REM length remains 229 bp (±235 bp) after aggregation, consistent with the published Roadmap (110 samples) + Blueprint (56 samples) build across 46 tissues and cell types

## Limitations

- The linear model used in STITCHIT, which forms the basis for assessing the Cell type score, is a simplification of the actual regulation of genes by their REMs
- CREMs are defined structurally (overlap or direct adjacency, minimum two REMs) rather than functionally, so a CREM boundary does not imply a single functional regulatory unit
- No tool versions are stated for STITCHIT/EpiRegio in the article, complicating exact reproduction
