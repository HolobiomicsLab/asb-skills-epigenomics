# bootstrap-null-distribution-analysis

## Summary

Quantify enrichment of overlapping genomic ranges by standardizing an observed statistic (e.g., overlap rate or spline-fitted overlap count) against a block-bootstrap null distribution generated with bootRanges, yielding a z-score instead of relying on shuffling or permutation p-values.

## When to use

Use when testing whether an observed overlap between genomic range sets (e.g., caQTL SNPs and peaks, or DEGs and differential accessibility peaks) is significant, and shuffling-based nulls may be too narrow.

## When NOT to use

- Shuffling or permutation nulls are acceptable because local genomic correlation structure is not a concern — the paper shows such schemes may yield overly narrow null distributions and over-estimated significance, which is exactly what this skill avoids.
- The analysis involves conventional genomic-feature shuffling workflows such as the bootstrap.R conventional-shuffling step rather than block-bootstrap nulls.

## Inputs

- Observed genomic ranges as GRanges objects (e.g., caQTL/total-cholesterol SNPs and liver peaks; or DEG logFC and differential accessibility peaks from the Alasoo 2018 macrophage dataset)
- Block-bootstrapped null range sets generated by bootRanges (output of segBootranges.R)

## Outputs

- Per-bootstrap-iteration overlap rates, across-replicate variance, and z-score written to overlap_rate_zscore.tsv
- Data-driven significance thresholds (SNPs -log10(P) > 8, genes |logFC| > 2) written to fitted_thresholds.csv

## How to apply

Construct GRanges objects for observed and bootstrapped range sets with nullranges/plyranges after the segBootranges.R step. For the liver caQTL/total-cholesterol analysis, compute per-bootstrap-iteration the overlap rate as the proportion of SNPs with a peak within 10 kb, pool the Lb rates, and compute their variance; standardize the observed rate as (observed rate - mean null rate) / SD of null rates, writing rates, variance, and z-score to overlap_rate_zscore.tsv. For threshold optimization (macrophage data), fit penalized-spline (P-spline) GLMs to observed overlap counts over logFC, fit the same GLMs to each bootRanges null set to build the null envelope, and recover the thresholds where the observed spline diverges — the paper recovered SNPs -log10(P-value) > 8 and genes |logFC| > 2. The rationale is that block bootstrap preserves local genomic correlation structure, generating more reliable null distributions than shuffling/permutation.

## Related tools

- **bootRanges** (Generates block-bootstrapped genomic ranges representing the null hypothesis for enrichment analysis; null sets feed the overlap-rate/z-score and spline-threshold computations.) — https://bioconductor.org/packages/nullranges
- **nullranges** (R/Bioconductor package that hosts bootRanges; used with plyranges to load observed and bootstrapped range sets as GRanges objects.) — https://bioconductor.org/packages/nullranges
- **GSC** (Genome-scale statistics approach whose running time was compared against bootRanges, not part of the null-generation workflow itself.)

## Evaluation signals

- Per-iteration overlap rates are computed as the proportion of SNPs with a peak within a 10 kb window, divided by total SNP count
- The z-score equals (observed overlap rate - mean of Lb null rates) / SD of null rates, and all three quantities appear in overlap_rate_zscore.tsv
- Recovered spline thresholds equal SNPs -log10(P-value) > 8 and genes |logFC| > 2, written to fitted_thresholds.csv
- Observed statistic lies well outside the bootRanges null distribution (strong z-score separation), unlike what overly narrow shuffling-based nulls would suggest

## Limitations

- Shuffling or permutation schemes may result in overly narrow test statistic null distributions and over-estimation of statistical significance, which is why block bootstrap is preferred but also means this skill is inappropriate when such nulls are the intended method.
- The statistic compared via the z score is specifically the overlap rate (proportion of SNPs with peaks within 10 kb); other statistics require redefining the per-iteration computation.
